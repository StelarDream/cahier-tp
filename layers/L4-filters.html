Filters & Bode

Transfer function \(H(\omega)\), gain, phase, cutoff at \(-3\,\text{dB}\). Bode diagrams.

Conventions on this page — \(\underline{H}(\omega) = \underline{U}_s/\underline{U}_e\) throughout. Normalised variable \(x = \omega/\omega_0 = f/f_c\). All transfer functions derived as voltage dividers with complex impedances from L3. DC/HF limits use open/short circuit rules from L2.

§1 — The Bode Diagram

Why decibels and log scale

When filters are cascaded, their gains multiply: \(G_{v,\text{total}} = G_{v,1} \cdot G_{v,2} \cdots\). Taking logarithms turns multiplication into addition:

$$g_{dB} = 20\log_{10}(G_v) \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad g_{dB,\text{total}} = g_{dB,1} + g_{dB,2} + \cdots$$
decibel definition — cascaded gains add

The frequency axis is logarithmic because circuits respond to ratios of frequency, not absolute differences. One decade is a factor of 10 in frequency. The slopes of filter asymptotes are always quoted in dB per decade.

Structure of the diagram

A Bode diagram has two panels sharing the same log-frequency axis:

  • Top: \(g_{dB}(f) = 20\log|\underline{H}(\omega)|\) — gain in dB
  • Bottom: \(\Delta\varphi(f) = \arg(\underline{H}(\omega))\) — phase shift in degrees

Asymptote method

The exact Bode curve is approximated by straight-line asymptotes — this is what you draw by hand. The rules are simple:

  • Each first-order factor \((1 + jx)\) in the denominator contributes a \(-20\,\text{dB/decade}\) slope above its corner frequency and \(-45°\) of phase at that frequency.
  • Each first-order factor in the numerator contributes \(+20\,\text{dB/decade}\) above its corner frequency and \(+45°\) of phase.
  • Second-order factors contribute \(\pm 40\,\text{dB/decade}\).
  • At the corner frequency: the exact gain is \(-3\,\text{dB}\) below the asymptote intersection.

§2 — First-Order Filters

All four canonical first-order filters are voltage dividers. The cutoff frequency is always \(f_c = 1/(2\pi\tau)\) where \(\tau\) is the time constant of the circuit.

Filter Circuit \(\underline{H}(\omega)\) \(f_c\) Slope (stopband) \(\Delta\varphi\) at \(f_c\)
RC low-pass R series, C output \(\dfrac{1}{1+jx}\) \(\dfrac{1}{2\pi RC}\) \(-20\,\text{dB/dec}\) \(-45°\)
RC high-pass C series, R output \(\dfrac{jx}{1+jx}\) \(\dfrac{1}{2\pi RC}\) \(+20\,\text{dB/dec}\) \(+45°\)
RL low-pass L series, R output \(\dfrac{1}{1+jx}\) \(\dfrac{R}{2\pi L}\) \(-20\,\text{dB/dec}\) \(-45°\)
RL high-pass R series, L output \(\dfrac{jx}{1+jx}\) \(\dfrac{R}{2\pi L}\) \(+20\,\text{dB/dec}\) \(+45°\)

Where \(x = \omega/\omega_c = f/f_c\) in all cases. The gain and phase for any first-order filter follow directly:

$$G_v = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^2}} \quad \text{(low-pass)} \qquad G_v = \frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}} \quad \text{(high-pass)}$$
first-order gain — low-pass and high-pass
$$\Delta\varphi = -\arctan(x) \quad \text{(low-pass)} \qquad \Delta\varphi = \frac{\pi}{2} - \arctan(x) \quad \text{(high-pass)}$$
first-order phase — low-pass and high-pass

Derivation: RC low-pass

Voltage divider, output across \(\underline{Z}_C\):

$$\underline{H} = \frac{\underline{Z}_C}{\underline{Z}_R + \underline{Z}_C} = \frac{1/jC\omega}{R + 1/jC\omega} = \frac{1}{1 + jRC\omega} = \frac{1}{1+jx} \quad \text{with } x = RC\omega$$
RC low-pass derivation

DC limit (\(\omega \to 0\)): \(\underline{Z}_C \to \infty\) → C is open circuit → output equals input → \(|\underline{H}| = 1\), \(g_{dB} = 0\). HF limit (\(\omega \to \infty\)): \(\underline{Z}_C \to 0\) → C is short circuit → output is shorted to ground → \(|\underline{H}| = 0\). Low-pass confirmed.

Derivation: RC high-pass

Voltage divider, output across \(\underline{Z}_R\):

$$\underline{H} = \frac{\underline{Z}_R}{\underline{Z}_R + \underline{Z}_C} = \frac{R}{R + 1/jC\omega} = \frac{jRC\omega}{1 + jRC\omega} = \frac{jx}{1+jx}$$
RC high-pass derivation

DC limit: \(\underline{Z}_C \to \infty\) → C is open circuit → no current flows → \(U_R = 0\) → \(|\underline{H}| = 0\). HF limit: \(\underline{Z}_C \to 0\) → C is short circuit → output equals input → \(|\underline{H}| = 1\). High-pass confirmed.

§3 — Loading Effect

The voltage divider formula assumes no current is drawn from the output node — \(i_s = 0\) (see L1 §5). In practice, whatever circuit follows the filter draws current, and this modifies the transfer function.

For the RC high-pass with a load \(R_L\) across the output, \(\underline{Z}_R\) becomes \(R \| R_L = RR_L/(R+R_L)\) and the new transfer function is:

$$\underline{H}'(\omega) = \frac{R\|R_L}{R\|R_L + \underline{Z}_C} \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad f_c' = \frac{1}{2\pi C (R\|R_L + R)} \neq f_c$$
RC high-pass with load — shifted cutoff frequency

The cutoff frequency shifts and the passband gain drops below 1. The smaller \(R_L\) is, the worse the distortion. This is the core motivation for the op-amp follower in L5: an ideal follower has infinite input impedance (draws no current) and zero output impedance (can drive any load without affecting the filter properties).

§4 — Second-Order Filters

Cascaded RC stages — transfer matrix method

Two RC low-pass stages in series (TP5). Each stage has transfer matrix \(T_{RC} = \begin{pmatrix} j\omega RC + 1 & R \\ j\omega C & 1 \end{pmatrix}\). The total matrix is \(T_\text{tot} = T_{RC}^2\), and \(\underline{H} = 1/a\) where \(a\) is the top-left element:

$$\underline{H}(\omega) = \frac{1}{1 - (\omega RC)^2 + 3j\omega RC}$$
RC-RC low-pass — transfer matrix method

The cutoff frequency is found by solving \(|\underline{H}(\omega_c)| = 1/\sqrt{2}\), which gives \(\omega_c = \sqrt{0.14}\,\omega_0 \approx 0.37\,\omega_0\) with \(\omega_0 = 1/RC\). The slope in the stopband is \(-40\,\text{dB/decade}\) — double that of a first-order filter.

RLC series — canonical second-order

A series RLC circuit driven by \(u_e\). Three possible outputs; three different filter types:

Output\(\underline{H}(\omega)\)Filter typeGain at \(\omega_0\)
Across R \(\dfrac{jx/Q}{1-x^2+jx/Q}\) Band-pass \(1\) (0 dB)
Across C \(\dfrac{1}{1-x^2+jx/Q}\) Low-pass (with resonance if \(Q > 1/\sqrt{2}\)) \(Q\) (\(+20\log Q\) dB)
Across L \(\dfrac{-x^2}{1-x^2+jx/Q}\) High-pass \(Q\)

Where \(x = \omega/\omega_0\), \(\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}\), and \(Q = \omega_0 L/R = (1/R)\sqrt{L/C}\).

§5 — Resonance and Bandwidth

Resonance in intensity (band-pass)

For the output across R, resonance always occurs at \(\omega = \omega_0\) regardless of \(Q\). The maximum gain is always 1 (0 dB). The bandwidth is:

$$\Delta f = f_2 - f_1 = \frac{f_0}{Q} \qquad \Leftrightarrow \qquad \Delta\omega = \frac{\omega_0}{Q} = \frac{R}{L}$$
RLC band-pass bandwidth

High \(Q\) → narrow bandwidth, sharp resonance. Low \(Q\) → broad, flat response. The two cutoff frequencies satisfy \(f_1 f_2 = f_0^2\) (geometric mean).

Surtension across C

For the output across C, resonance only exists if \(Q > 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.707\). When it does, the maximum voltage across C exceeds the source voltage — this is the surtension:

$$U_{C,\text{max}} = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{1 - 1/(4Q^2)}}\, U_0 \approx Q\, U_0 \quad \text{for large } Q$$
surtension — peak voltage across C exceeds source
Energy interpretation: at resonance, energy sloshes back and forth between the electric field in C (\(E_C = \frac{1}{2}CU_C^2\)) and the magnetic field in L (\(E_L = \frac{1}{2}LI^2\)) — exactly like a mechanical oscillator exchanging kinetic and potential energy. R is the damper. \(Q\) measures how many cycles the energy survives before being dissipated.

§6 — Filter Identification Checklist

A systematic procedure for any filter problem in the exam:

StepActionWhat you learn
1 — DC limit Replace C → open circuit, L → short circuit. Read \(U_s/U_e\). Output at \(f = 0\). Zero → high-pass or band-pass. \(U_e\) → low-pass or band-pass.
2 — HF limit Replace C → short circuit, L → open circuit. Read \(U_s/U_e\). Output at \(f \to \infty\). Zero → low-pass or band-pass. \(U_e\) → high-pass.
3 — Identify type DC=1, HF=0 → low-pass. DC=0, HF=1 → high-pass. DC=0, HF=0 → band-pass. Filter type confirmed.
4 — Write \(\underline{H}\) Apply voltage divider with complex \(\underline{Z}\). Simplify. Exact transfer function.
5 — Factor Write as \(H_\text{max} \cdot f(x)\) with \(x = \omega/\omega_0\). Read \(\omega_0\), \(Q\). \(f_c\) or \(f_0\), \(Q\), maximum gain.
6 — Sketch Draw asymptotes. Mark \(f_c\). Note \(-3\,\text{dB}\) at corner. Add phase. Bode diagram complete.
